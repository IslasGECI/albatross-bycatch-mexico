
# Methods

## Study Area

<!-- main idea: M1 Study area — the spatial frame: the EEZ, the three colonies, the protected areas involved, and the oceanography that places the core areas. Point: "Specifically, we assessed whether Mexico's MPAs encompass core areas used by the Laysan albatross during its breeding season." -->
We studied the core areas used by Laysan albatross within Mexico's EEZ.
Specifically, we assessed whether Mexico's MPAs encompass core areas used by the Laysan albatross during its breeding season.
The MPAs of interest for this study corresponded to the marine components of three PNAs.
Guadalupe Island Biosphere Reserve and Revillagigedo National Park hosted the three albatross colonies.
The Pacific Islands of the Baja California Peninsula Biosphere Reserve lay along the foraging paths used by the albatross during its breeding season.

<!-- point: "The oceanographic conditions of the waters surrounding the breeding colonies determine foraging patterns, which in turn define the albatross core areas." -->
The oceanographic conditions of the waters surrounding the breeding colonies determine foraging patterns, which in turn define the albatross core areas.
The California Current and the El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) phenomenon are examples of such oceanographic conditions.

<!-- point: none — the sentence announces the three island descriptions that follow. -->
Below we describe the three islands where the colonies breed.

### Guadalupe Island
<!-- point: "Guadalupe Island hosts the largest breeding colony of Laysan albatross in the eastern Pacific [@hernandez2019sexual]." -->
Guadalupe Island hosts the largest breeding colony of Laysan albatross in the eastern Pacific [@hernandez2019sexual].
On Guadalupe Island, there were 4,390 breeding individuals in 2025 (unpublished authors' monitoring data).
Guadalupe Island is an oceanic island of volcanic origin located 300 km west of the coast of Baja California.
It has an area of 244 km$^2$.
It measures 30 km at its longest part from the southern end to the northern end.

### Clarion Island
<!-- point: "Clarion Island has the second largest Laysan albatross colony in Mexico." -->
Clarion Island is part of the Revillagigedo Archipelago.
Clarion is located 700 km from the Mexican mainland.
It has a tropical climate.
It has an area of 19.8 km$^2$.
It measures 8.6 km at its longest part from the eastern end to the western end.
Clarion Island has the second largest Laysan albatross colony in Mexico.
On Clarion Island, crows and snakes prey on Laysan albatross eggs and newly hatched chicks.
The colony experienced complete breeding failure between 2016 and 2023 [@castillo2026breeding].
The colony held approximately 100 breeding individuals in 2021/22 [@castillo2026breeding].

### San Benedicto
<!-- point: "Reproductive success for Laysan albatross is close to 0%." -->
San Benedicto Island is also part of the Revillagigedo Archipelago and shares the same tropical climate.
San Benedicto is located 600 km from the Mexican mainland.
It has an area of 10 km$^2$.
It measures 4.8 km at its longest part from the northern end to the southern end.
On San Benedicto Island, crabs feed on the chicks, and nests can be buried in ash.
The colony holds fewer than 80 breeding individuals.
Reproductive success for Laysan albatross is close to 0%.

## GPS deployment

<!-- main idea: M2 GPS deployment — capture, attachment, and recovery; the breeding-season window and why devices return within it; the devices and settings across years and islands. Point: "This ensured that we could recover the GPS devices before the individuals migrated, as recovery would not have been possible the next breeding season." -->

We deployed and recovered GPS tracking devices while the individuals were breeding on the island.
The devices were deployed from December to June, covering the breeding season, which generally spans from November to August.
This ensured that we could recover the GPS devices before the individuals migrated, as recovery would not have been possible the next breeding season.

<!-- point: "No albatross were harmed during the study." -->
No albatross were harmed during the study.
The seabirds were captured by hand at their nests, and the GPS devices were attached to back feathers shed during the non-breeding season.
We attached the GPS loggers using Tesa tape (#4651, Tesa AG, Hamburg, Germany), following the method described in [@hernandez2019sexual].
The individuals were released at the same location immediately after the GPS device was attached or removed.

<!-- point: "This frequency is high enough to obtain representative data and to identify instances where an individual stops or reduces its speed." and "At the same time, it is low enough to ensure the battery lasts for the entire season, allowing for the recording of several trips." -->
The GPS loggers offer the level of accuracy and precision required for this study [@soanes2013many; @clay2023adjustment; @hernandez2019sexual].
We programmed the GPS units to record a position every 10 minutes.
This frequency is high enough to obtain representative data and to identify instances where an individual stops or reduces its speed.
At the same time, it is low enough to ensure the battery lasts for the entire season, allowing for the recording of several trips.

## Data Analysis

### Core areas used by Laysan albatross

<!-- main idea: M4 Overview — why track2KBA in two sentences, the six steps without function names, the promise of detail, and the archived analysis code. Point: "We followed the track2KBA methodology because it is replicable using a single open-source R package [@beal2021track2kba]." -->
We followed the track2KBA methodology because it is replicable using a single open-source R package [@beal2021track2kba].
Also, it is explicitly designed to identify important sites for biodiversity from tracking data [@beal2021track2kba].
We consolidated the GPS records and split them into individual foraging trips.
We removed incomplete trips and trips that never reached beyond 60 km of the colony.
We fitted kernel density estimates for each individual.
We assessed the sample representativeness.
We identified the area used by a threshold percentage of the population.
We defined the core area.
Below, we describe these steps in more detail.
We shared the full analysis code on Zenodo at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23485336.

<!-- main idea: M5 Preparation — consolidate the records into one database, split the tracks into trips, remove incomplete trips and trips that never reached beyond 60 km, and keep the survivors as the foraging trips. Point: "The remaining trips were considered foraging trips." -->
The remaining trips were considered foraging trips.
We consolidated the GPS records to bring them into a single database with a common format.
We split all tracks into single trips.
We removed incomplete trips, which are those without a recorded return to the colony.
Following @hernandez2019sexual, we removed trips that never reached beyond 60 km of the colony.

<!-- main idea: M6 KDE — the ARS scale from First Passage Time is the KDE smoothing parameter; a 50% UD holds half the probability mass of a KDE, one per individual. Point: "We calculated one 50% UD for each individual albatross [@beal2021track2kba]." -->
We calculated one 50% UD for each individual albatross [@beal2021track2kba].
The area-restricted search (ARS) scale is estimated using first-passage time analysis (adehabitatLT).
We used that scale as the smoothing parameter of the KDEs.
The 50% UD is the region that contains half of the probability mass of the KDE.

<!-- main idea: M7 Trust — what the representativeness assessment answers; the KDEs of a resampled subset of individuals averaged into a UD; the inclusion of the locations left out; the threshold percentage it sets for the foraging component. Point: "The representativeness assessment indicates whether the areas identified as important for the sample are valid for the entire population." -->
The representativeness assessment indicates whether the areas identified as important for the sample are valid for the entire population.
The representativeness assessment averages the kernel density estimates (KDEs) of a resampled subset of individuals to obtain a utilization distribution (UD).
It measures the inclusion of locations from the individuals not in the subsample.
It also sets the threshold percentage of the population that we use to define the foraging component.

<!-- main idea: M8 Define — the foraging component is the cells whose individual 50% UDs reach the threshold set by the assessment; the core area is its union with the 60 km disk. Point: "The foraging component is the cells where the individual 50% UDs overlap enough to reach the threshold set by the representativeness assessment." -->
The foraging component is the cells where the individual 50% UDs overlap enough to reach the threshold set by the representativeness assessment.
We define the core area as the union of the foraging component and the disk with a radius of 60 km around each colony.

### Overlap of core areas with MPAs

<!-- main idea: M9 Place and Measure — the overlap with the existing MPAs; the index formula; the core-area size against the size of its overlap; the fraction inside; the meaning of 1 and 0. Point: "This index tells us what fraction of Laysan albatross core areas is inside the MPAs." -->

Once the core areas were identified, we quantified their overlap with the existing MPAs using the overlap index [@fieberg2005quantifying].
The overlap index is defined as $HR_{i,j} = \frac{A_{i,j}}{A_i}$ and is directional ($HR_{i,j} \neq HR_{j,i}$).
In our case, $A_i$ is the core-area size, and $A_{i,j}$ is the size of its overlap with the MPAs.
A value of 1 indicates that the core area is entirely inside the MPAs, while a value of 0 indicates that it is entirely outside.
This index tells us what fraction of Laysan albatross core areas is inside the MPAs.
