
# Introduction

<!-- point: seabirds among the most threatened groups of birds and the causes of their decline: bycatch, introduced predators, harvesting, and overfishing. -->
Seabirds are one of the most threatened groups of birds, with ~31% of species at risk and ~47% in decline [@dias2019threats].
Bycatch [@birdlife2018phoebastria; @naughton2007conservation; @safina1995world; @mendez2022population], introduced predators [@birdlife2018phoebastria; @naughton2007conservation; @mendez2022population], and feather and egg harvesting [@birdlife2018phoebastria; @naughton2007conservation] have reduced seabird numbers, and overfishing has depleted their prey [@safina1995world].

<!-- point: MPAs are an important conservation tool, but they are coastal and colony-centred, so open-ocean users are missed by design: 13.2% of pelagic populations fall inside MPAs against 32.5% of coastal ones. -->
In light of all these negative impacts, various strategies have been implemented to prevent the collapse of seabird populations [@naughton2007conservation; @mendez2022population; @dias2019threats; @phillips2024incidental].
In particular, marine protected areas (MPAs) are considered an important tool for the conservation of seabirds [@critchley2018marine].
However, protected areas for seabirds usually extend from the coast around a significant colony [@critchley2018marine].
Pelagic species, such as albatrosses, receive less protection through MPAs than coastal species do [@lascelles2012hotspots; @critchley2018marine].
13.2% of pelagic populations are covered by MPAs, compared to 32.5% of coastal populations [@critchley2018marine].

<!-- point: Laysan albatrosses nesting on Guadalupe, Clarion, and San Benedicto; Guadalupe lies in the Guadalupe Island Biosphere Reserve and the other two in the Revillagigedo National Park; the islands and their surrounding waters lie within protected areas; commercial fishing takes place in the waters adjacent to these protected areas. -->
In Mexico, the Laysan albatross nests on the islands of Guadalupe, Clarion, and San Benedicto [@pitman2004population].
All three islands are located within protected areas, and their surrounding waters are MPAs [@castillo2026breeding; @mendez2022population].
Guadalupe Island is part of the Guadalupe Island Biosphere Reserve, while Clarion and San Benedicto are part of the Revillagigedo National Park [@mendez2022population; @castillo2026breeding].
The waters adjacent to these MPAs are subject to commercial fishing.

<!-- point: to determine whether MPAs cover the areas that matter for a species, those areas must first be identified; GPS tracking is the standard method for identifying them; such areas should guide MPA delineation; in the Mexican Pacific, protected areas are linked to terrestrial units and were not delineated from seabird tracking. -->
We must first identify areas that are important for a given species to determine whether MPAs cover those areas.
Tracking seabirds, for example using GPS devices, is a standard method for identifying such important marine areas [@lascelles2016applying].
Ideally, these areas should serve as the basis for delineating marine protected areas [@lascelles2016applying; @critchley2018marine].
However, in the Mexican Pacific, protected areas were not delineated based on seabird tracking data [@mendez2022population].

<!-- point: no study has quantified how much of the Laysan albatross core area in the Mexican Pacific falls inside existing MPAs. -->
No study has quantified what proportion of the areas important for Laysan albatross in the Mexican Pacific falls within MPAs.
Without this information, we cannot determine whether the MPAs are spatially adequate.

<!-- point: first 12-year, three-colony identification of the Laysan albatross core area in Mexico; the prior baseline is five years at Guadalupe and four at Clarion; this study incorporates and extends those data; track2KBA had not been applied to the species in Mexico. -->
This study is the first to use {{n_tracking_span_years}} years of GPS tracking data from three colonies to identify Laysan albatross core areas in Mexico.
@hernandez2019sexual identified areas based on a 5-year time series from the colony on Guadalupe Island.
@castillo2026breeding identified areas based on a 4-year time series from the colony on Clarion Island.
This study incorporates and extends the data from these previous studies.
Furthermore, this study is the first to employ *track2KBA* [@beal2021track2kba] to identify the core areas of the Laysan albatross.

<!-- point: two questions, where the Laysan albatross core areas lie in the Mexican Pacific and what proportion falls inside MPAs; the hypothesis that the proportion is low because the MPAs were not designed around those areas; the approach that delineates the core area from pooled GPS tracking and measures its overlap with the MPA layer. -->
We ask two questions: where are the Laysan albatross core areas in the Mexican Pacific, and what proportion of them falls inside MPAs?
Given that the MPAs were not designed with these core areas in mind, we hypothesize that this proportion will be low.
To answer them, we delineate the core area from pooled GPS tracking and measure its overlap with the MPA layer.
