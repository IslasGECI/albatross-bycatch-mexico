# STORY

- OPENING — Seabirds are among the most threatened groups of birds, and the tool for protecting them at sea is anchored to land: pelagic species are missed by design. This is the problem the paper opens and closes on.
- CHALLENGE — No study has quantified how much of the Laysan albatross core area in the Mexican Pacific falls inside existing MPAs. The paper asks where that core area lies and how much of it the MPAs cover, hypothesizes limited coverage, and adds a brief approach clause: delineate the core area from pooled GPS tracking and the colony rings, and measure its overlap with the MPA layer. Core area means the union of the potential KBA and the colony rings; the potential KBA alone does not carry the reported percentage. Every Introduction paragraph must drive toward those questions; detailed umbrella, oceanography, replication, and sea-use material lives outside the funnel, while single motivating clauses stay inside it.
- ACTION — The Action runs Trust, Define, Place, Measure: tagged and analyzed individuals from Guadalupe, Clarion, and San Benedicto across the breeding seasons are shown representative; the individual 50% UDs delineate the potential KBA, which unites with the colony rings to form the core area; that core area is set against the spatial frame (EEZ plus the MPA layer); and its overlap with MPAs is quantified on the union basis.
- RESOLUTION — The headline is a single percentage on the union basis, where the same union appears on both sides so overlapping rings and the potential KBA cannot be double-counted. The number's meaning is carried by location, not magnitude: protection is anchored to islands while the core area lies offshore beyond every boundary, so the pelagic gap reproduces as a spatial pattern. The Discussion answers the questions, closes the Opening circle spatially with the global coverage study cited qualitatively, limits the claim to the breeding season, and presents the potential KBA and the core area as layers available for future spatial planning; the Conclusion is a short resolution paragraph that restates the percentage once and closes the Opening circle.

## INTRODUCTION — Opening then Challenge; funnel, 8 beats; the questions and hypothesis land in the final beat

- I1 Hook — seabirds among the most threatened groups of birds, and the causes of their decline.
- I2 Pelagic gap — MPAs are a conservation tool, but they sit on the coast, so pelagic species receive less protection.
- I3 Mexican case — the Laysan albatross nests on Guadalupe, Clarion, and San Benedicto; the islands and their waters sit in protected areas; fishing works those waters.
- I4 Why tracking — the areas must be located before coverage can be tested, and tracking locates them.
- I5 Concepts — fix the core-area term, distinguish MPA from the broader protected-area term, and seed the Key Biodiversity Area term; full definitions stay in Methods.
- I6 The gap — no study has quantified how much of the Laysan albatross core area in the Mexican Pacific falls inside existing MPAs.
- I7 Novelty — the first pooled core-area analysis for Mexico's albatross colonies.
- I8 Question + hypothesis + approach — where the core area lies and how much the MPAs cover; the hypothesis of limited coverage; the mapped overlap from pooled tracking.
