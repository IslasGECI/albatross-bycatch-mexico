# STORY

- OPENING — Seabirds are among the most threatened groups of birds, and the tool for protecting them at sea is anchored to land: pelagic species are missed by design. This is the problem the paper opens and closes on.
- CHALLENGE — No study has quantified how much of the Laysan albatross core area in the Mexican Pacific falls inside existing MPAs. The paper asks where that core area lies and how much of it the MPAs cover, hypothesizes limited coverage, and adds a brief approach clause: delineate the core area from pooled GPS tracking and the 60 km radius, and measure its overlap with the MPA layer. Core area means the union of the foraging component and the 60 km radius; the foraging component alone does not carry the reported percentage. Every Introduction paragraph must drive toward those questions; detailed umbrella, oceanography, replication, and sea-use material lives outside the funnel, while single motivating clauses stay inside it.
- ACTION — The Action runs preparation and KDE, Trust, Define, Place, Measure: the KDEs give each individual's 50% UD, and the tracked sample from the three colonies is shown representative; the UDs delineate the foraging component, which unites with the area within a 60 km radius to form the core area; that core area is set against the spatial frame (EEZ plus the MPA layer); and its overlap with MPAs is quantified on the union basis.
- RESOLUTION — The headline is a single percentage on the union basis, where the same union appears on both sides so the area within a 60 km radius and the foraging component cannot be double-counted. The number's meaning is carried by location, not magnitude: protection is anchored to islands while the core area lies offshore beyond every boundary, so the pelagic gap reproduces as a spatial pattern. The Discussion answers the questions, closes the Opening circle spatially with the global coverage study cited qualitatively, limits the claim to the breeding season, and presents the foraging component and the core area as layers available for future spatial planning; the Conclusion is a short resolution paragraph that restates the percentage once and closes the Opening circle.

## INTRODUCTION — Opening then Challenge; funnel, 8 beats; the questions and hypothesis land in the final beat

- I1 Hook — seabirds among the most threatened groups of birds, and the causes of their decline.
- I2 Pelagic gap — MPAs are a conservation tool, but they sit on the coast, so pelagic species receive less protection.
- I3 Mexican case — the Laysan albatross nests on Guadalupe, Clarion, and San Benedicto; the islands and their waters sit in protected areas; fishing works those waters.
- I4 Why tracking — the areas must be located before coverage can be tested, and tracking locates them.
- I5 Concepts — fix the core-area term and distinguish MPA from the broader protected-area term; full definitions stay in Methods.
- I6 The gap — no study has quantified how much of the Laysan albatross core area in the Mexican Pacific falls inside existing MPAs.
- I7 Novelty — the first pooled core-area analysis for Mexico's albatross colonies.
- I8 Question + hypothesis + approach — where the core area lies and how much the MPAs cover; the hypothesis of limited coverage; the mapped overlap from pooled tracking.

## METHODS — Action (the doing); 10 beats, ordered preparation and KDE → Trust → Define → Place → Measure

- M1 Study area — the spatial frame: the EEZ, the three colonies, the protected areas involved, and the oceanography that places the core areas.
- M2 GPS deployment — capture, attachment, and recovery; the breeding-season window and why devices return within it; the devices and settings across years and islands.
- M3 Tracking data — tagged individuals per colony across the breeding seasons; trips; effort concentrated at the largest colony; the long series and the comparable seasons.
- M3.5 Overview — the workflow in six numbered steps without function names; the detail follows in the same order; the archived analysis code.
- M4 Preparation and KDE — the records consolidated; the tracks split into trips; incomplete trips and trips that stayed within 60 km removed; the ARS scale as the KDE smoothing parameter; the 50% UDs per individual; no resampling.
- M5 Trust — the representativeness assessment: whether the sample's areas hold for the population; the KDEs averaged over a resampled subset; the inclusion of the locations left out; the threshold it sets.
- M6 Define — the foraging component as the cells whose 50% UDs reach that threshold; the core area as its union with the 60 km disk.
- M7 Place — the MPA layer inside the EEZ mask; the intersection arithmetic and its units.
- M8 Measure — the overlap index on the union basis; why the area within a 60 km radius enters both sides.
- M9 Ethical considerations — permits, minimization, no harm.

## DISCUSSION — Resolution; 4 moves, ordered answer → limitation → circle → contribution

- D1 Answer — the questions answered: the percentage and the spatial answer, the core area offshore while coverage hugs islands.
- D2 Limitation — the data cover the breeding season only; the conclusions apply there, which is also the most relevant period.
- D3 Circle — the aggregate hides placement: protection is anchored to islands while the core area sits beyond every boundary; the global coverage study is cited qualitatively.
- D4 Contribution — the paper evaluates existing MPAs rather than proposing new areas; island-anchored protection leaves the pelagic core area uncovered; the foraging-component layer is subordinate but available for planning.
