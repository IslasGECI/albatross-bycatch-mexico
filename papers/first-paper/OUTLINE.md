# STORY

- OPENING — Seabirds are among the most threatened groups of birds, and the main tool for protecting them at sea is anchored to land: 13.2% of pelagic species fall inside MPAs against 32.5% of coastal ones, so open-ocean users are missed by design. This is the problem the paper opens and closes on.
- CHALLENGE — No study has quantified how much of the Laysan albatross core area in the Mexican Pacific falls inside existing MPAs. The paper asks one question — do Mexico's MPAs cover that core area? — states the hypothesis that they do not, and adds a brief approach clause (delineate the core area from 12 years of pooled GPS tracking; measure its overlap with the MPA layer). Core area here means the union of the offshore 50% UD polygons and the 60 km colony rings; the offshore component alone is a methodological byproduct and is not reported. Every Introduction paragraph must drive toward this question; detailed umbrella, oceanography, replication, and sea-use/stakeholder sections live outside the funnel, while single motivating clauses (the longline fleet, the KBA seed) stay inside it.
- ACTION — The Action runs Trust, Define, Place, Measure: N_TAGGED tagged and N_ANALYZED analyzed individuals from Guadalupe, Clarion, and San Benedicto across 12 breeding seasons (2014–2026) are shown representative (REPRESENTATIVENESS inclusion, Rep95 = REP95; the 2014–2018 five-year claim stays qualitative); a pooled 50% UD delineates the offshore component, which unites with the 60 km colony rings to form the core area; that core area is set against the spatial frame (EEZ plus the MPA layer); and its overlap with MPAs is quantified on the union basis. Figure order is locked (tracks, representativeness, N_IND surface, core area with MPAs); only the prose moves.
- RESOLUTION — The headline is a single percentage, CORE_MPA_PCT, on the union basis ((core ∪ rings) ∩ MPA) / (core ∪ rings); the same union appears on both sides, so overlapping rings and offshore component cannot be double-counted. The number's meaning is carried by location, not magnitude: the protection is anchored to islands (Revillagigedo's zones, the coastal band) while the core area lies offshore beyond every boundary (Fig 4), so the pelagic gap reproduces as a spatial pattern. The Discussion answers the question, closes the Opening circle spatially with the global coverage study cited qualitatively, limits the claim to the breeding season, and presents the core area as a candidate KBA that the layer makes available for future spatial planning; the Conclusion is a short resolution paragraph that restates the percentage once and closes the Opening circle.
